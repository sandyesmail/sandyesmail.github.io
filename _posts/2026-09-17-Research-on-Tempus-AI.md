# Tempus AI: Where DNA Meets Artificial Intelligence

### How sequencing technology is helping personalize cancer treatment

Imagine if doctors could look at the DNA of a patient's cancer and use that information to help decide which treatment may work best. That is one of the ideas behind **Tempus AI**, a healthcare technology company that combines genetic sequencing, medical data, and artificial intelligence to help make healthcare more personalized.

For this assignment, I chose Tempus because I think it is really interesting how sequencing can go beyond simply reading DNA. The genetic information collected through sequencing can actually be combined with a patient's medical information to help doctors better understand their cancer and treatment options.

---

## Who Founded Tempus?

**Founder:** Eric Lefkofsky
**Founded:** 2015
**Headquarters:** Chicago, Illinois

Tempus AI was founded by **Eric Lefkofsky in 2015**. His idea for the company came from a personal experience. After his wife was diagnosed with breast cancer, Lefkofsky noticed that hospitals had huge amounts of medical data, but much of it was not being organized and used effectively to help doctors make treatment decisions.

This inspired him to create Tempus and find a way to combine technology, genomic information, and clinical data to make medicine more personalized.

---

## From a Startup to a Billion-Dollar Company

Tempus received approximately **$10 million in its first Series A funding round in 2015**. Since then, the company has grown significantly and became publicly traded in **2024**.

| Year |             Revenue |
| ---- | ------------------: |
| 2024 |  About $693 million |
| 2025 | About $1.27 billion |

That means Tempus increased its annual revenue by more than **80% in just one year**.

Even with this growth, Tempus reported a **net loss of about $245 million in 2025**. This shows an important difference between revenue and profit. A company can bring in a large amount of revenue while still spending more money than it earns.

---

## What Does Tempus Actually Do?

The main focus of Tempus is **precision medicine**, which means using information about an individual patient to help personalize their medical care.

One major part of its business is **genetic testing and sequencing**. For cancer patients, Tempus can analyze **DNA and RNA** to identify genetic changes associated with their tumors. Doctors can combine these results with other clinical information when considering possible treatments.

### Sequencing → Data → AI → Personalized Medicine

Tempus also works with pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies. Its large collection of **de-identified clinical and molecular data** can be used for medical research and drug development.

Because of this, Tempus has several different parts to its business, including:

* Genetic and molecular testing
* Data and technology services
* Pharmaceutical research partnerships
* Clinical trial services

---

## Taking Sequencing Even Further

One development that caught my attention was the introduction of **Tempus xH**, the company's first whole-genome sequencing assay, in 2025.

Instead of examining only selected genes, **whole-genome sequencing (WGS)** can examine much more of a person's complete genetic information. This gives researchers and healthcare professionals access to a much larger amount of genomic data.

This is especially interesting in cancer research because cancers can contain many different genetic alterations. Having more genomic information could potentially help researchers better understand those differences.

---

## What Does the Future Look Like?

I think Tempus has a lot of potential because personalized medicine is becoming increasingly important in healthcare. The company is combining three rapidly developing areas:

### Genomics + Big Data + Artificial Intelligence

Its revenue growth shows increasing demand for its diagnostic and data services, and Tempus is continuing to expand its sequencing technology and AI tools.

However, its future success is not guaranteed. Even though Tempus generated approximately **$1.27 billion in revenue in 2025**, it still reported a net loss of about **$245 million**.

For Tempus to continue succeeding long-term, it will need to keep expanding its technology and genomic database while eventually controlling its costs enough to become consistently profitable.

---

## My Takeaway

What interested me most about Tempus is that sequencing is only the beginning of what the company does. Reading someone's DNA creates an enormous amount of information, but that information becomes much more useful when it can be connected with medical records and analyzed.

Tempus shows how **biology and computer science can work together**. Sequencing provides the genetic information, big data allows information from many patients to be studied, and AI can help researchers and healthcare professionals analyze those patterns.

To me, companies like Tempus show where the future of cancer research may be heading: toward treatments that are increasingly based on the biology of an individual patient's cancer rather than treating every patient's disease exactly the same way.
